Open items
- City data: the preview flies over a city generated in code (src/city); it reads as a 3D model, not a photo. The photorealistic route is Google's 3D Tiles: copy `.env.example` to `.env.local`, add a Google Maps Platform key with the Map Tiles API enabled, run `npm run dev`. It cannot load inside the preview page (network is blocked there).
- Real-city path, to do on first run with a key: check each latitude/longitude in `src/city/geo.ts` (typed from memory), tune `GROUND_Y` until altitude 0 meets the water, confirm the axes (east/south) and that the attribution line shows. Console tags, patrol routes and the airport fence are shifted with their landmark, not fitted to the real buildings. Day / Dusk does nothing there (the tiles carry their own daylight). Google bills tile usage.
- Performance: the owner's machine showed 60 fps in flight before the realism pass (his screenshot, 6 Oct). That pass added about 9,000 small boxes (roof plant, podiums), 3D traffic around the camera and several texture lookups per pixel for reflections and contact shade; frame rate after it has not been measured on real hardware. If it is too low, the cheapest cuts are, in order: the reflection loop in the water (12 steps in `makeGroundMaterial`), `POOL_LINES` in `street.ts`, and `roofGear` in `layout.ts`.
- The automated browser renders the new city at well under 1 fps (software rendering), so checks there take minutes per screen and say nothing about speed.
- City map (src/data/citymap.ts): station and expressway names are real; every arrival time, speed, alert, project, reading and count is invented demo data, and places sit where they fit the stand-in city, not at true coordinates. With real tiles each place and line needs real coordinates. On narrow screens the map has no markers: the places are listed in the panel instead.
- Flight has only been driven by keyboard in an automated browser at about 1 fps (software rendering). Mouse steering, pointer lock and real frame rate still need a check on real hardware.
- Country list: only Singapore, Indonesia and Saudi Arabia are confirmed; the rest are placeholders.
- Avatar models: GLB files to be supplied. To swap one: drop the file in public/avatars/ and set `model: 'avatars/<file>.glb'` on that entry in src/data/avatars.ts. Preview and roster thumbnail update on their own.
- Landmark console: every figure, team, person and work order is invented demo data (src/data/ops.ts). Camera tiles, replay and playback are stills of the 3D twin, not video; there is no recorded footage or match video. All alerts share the two drawn shapes (one fence, one tripwire) at airside gate 4.
- Console checked in an automated browser at 1280x720, 1000x600 and 400x800 (`node scripts/shot-console.mjs`). Camera moves between views run at about 1 fps there, so their feel still needs a look on real hardware.
- City statistics (src/data/tour.ts): figures follow Vizzio's Singapore showcase video with units corrected. They have not been checked against official sources and stay labelled demo data. Replace them before any public use.
- CCTV viewer: it shows the 3D twin from the camera's position, not video. IP address and resolution on the overlay are invented.
